How to photograph Nazaré

Where to stand, when to go and what to bring. What I learned in three years of coming and going.

Guide

Nazaré has two sides. North of the cliff is Praia do Norte, the open beach where the giant waves break. To the south, sheltered, is the town beach with its avenue, the boats and the Sítio above, linked by the funicular.

For the waves, everything happens in winter and depends on the forecast. For street and light, any season works: the sun sets over the sea all year round.

Frequently asked questions

When are the big waves in Nazaré?

The season runs from October to March. November, December, January and February are the months with the best chance of giant waves. From May to August the sea is usually calm.

Why are the waves in Nazaré so big?

Off Praia do Norte there is an underwater canyon that concentrates the energy of North Atlantic swells. When a large storm arrives, the wave grows as it enters the canyon.

Where is the best place to photograph the waves?

On the esplanade of the Forte de São Miguel Arcanjo, on top of the cliff. On big days it gets crowded and parking is hard, so arrive early.

What lens do I need?

For the surfers, a long telephoto between 400 and 600 mm, because many waves break far out. For the landscape and the scale of the place, a standard zoom is enough.

How do I know if there will be big waves?

Check the swell forecast: a period of 13 seconds or more, a west or northwest swell and a light east or southeast wind. A mid, rising tide often brings the strongest sets.

V

About me

Matías Silva, photographer between San Miguel and Nazaré.

I am from San Miguel, Buenos Aires. Nazaré is my second home.

I lived in Portugal for three years. Part of that time I was based in Nazaré, and the rest of the time I travelled there whenever I could to study its waves, its culture, its people and its energy. Those were three years of looking at the same place with patience, and that is where the way I photograph it today comes from.

I created Viva Nazaré, a project dedicated to the people, the culture and the giant waves of the town. Before that I co-founded Nazaré Inside, which publishes reports on the waves and local life.

I photograph the sea, the street and nature. I am preparing to return to Nazaré in the winter of 2027 to photograph the big wave season from the Forte. This website is the record of that journey.
